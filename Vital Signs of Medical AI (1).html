    Patient history, 1950 to 2022

    The background before the focus period, including both AI winters. Medical milestones are marked.

    1950s–60s · Birth

    • 1950Turing proposes the Turing Test.
    • 1956Dartmouth Conference names "artificial intelligence."
    • 1958Rosenblatt's Perceptron, an early neural network.
    • 1959Arthur Samuel coins "machine learning."
    • 1964–66ELIZA imitates a therapist.MED
    • 1969Perceptrons shows the limits of simple neural networks.

    1970s–80s · Winters

    • 1972MYCIN diagnoses blood infections, but never reaches hospitals.MED
    • 1974–1980First AI winter: weak computers, failed promises, funding cut.
    • 1980Expert systems like XCON go commercial.
    • 1986Backpropagation makes deep networks trainable.
    • 1987–1993Second AI winter: brittle expert systems, hardware market collapses.

    1990s–2000s · Data

    • 1997Deep Blue beats Kasparov at chess.
    • 1998FDA approves R2 ImageChecker for mammograms.MED
    • 2006Hinton revives deep learning; AWS launches the cloud.
    • 2009ImageNet and GPU training arrive.

    2010s–2022 · Boom

    • 2012AlexNet starts the deep learning era.
    • 2013IBM Watson for Oncology, later abandoned.MED
    • 2017Transformers introduced.
    • 2018FDA authorizes IDx-DR, the first autonomous diagnostic AI.MED
    • 2019Hospital algorithm found biased against Black patients.MED
    • 2020AlphaFold 2 solves protein structure prediction.MED
    • Nov 2022ChatGPT launches.

    Our reflection

    What stood out to us is how fast AI went from barely passing medical exams to beating doctors on hard cases, in just about three years. But the ethical events tell a different story. Passing a test isn't the same as treating a real patient safely, and cases like UnitedHealth show what happens when AI decisions replace human judgment.

    Coming from UX, accessibility, and data backgrounds, we think the real question isn't how smart the AI is, but who it's designed for and who is accountable when it gets it wrong.
