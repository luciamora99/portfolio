Leading as a learner.

AI moves faster than any degree can keep up with. The people who thrive are the ones who know exactly where they stand and what to learn next. At the start of my Master's in AI & Data Analytics, I took an honest inventory of my skills. This is what I found, and what I'm doing about it.

Written for: AI and data teams looking for someone who connects models, data, and the people who actually use them.

The assessment

Where I stand today.

Strong Strengths

  • Adapting to change: moving from Colombia to the U.S. taught me to adjust quickly to new environments.
  • Staying calm when plans shift: I balance a master's degree, an internship at Siemens, and practicing tennis, so changing plans are normal for me.
  • Learning fast: I'm learning Qlik from scratch at Siemens, and I ask for feedback often because I'd rather learn fast than pretend I know.
  • Ethics & fairness: I work on WCAG accessibility in our dashboards, making sure a tool works fairly for everyone. It's the same idea behind reducing bias in AI.
  • Business goals first: my business background helps me ask "why are we building this?" before jumping into the technical side.

Growing Focus areas

  • Delegation: on projects like selling ad space for a local ice rink or building my own app, I tend to do everything myself. I'm organized, but I need to trust others and share the work.
  • Explaining AI simply: on the tennis court I break things down for beginners easily, but with technical topics I get too detailed. I want to bring my coaching style into how I explain tech.
  • Hands-on leadership: I haven't led an AI project or trained a team on new AI tools yet, so some of my ratings reflect what I know more than what I've done.

What I learned about self-assessment

Self-assessments are a good starting point, but rating yourself only goes so far. Feedback from others would make the results more accurate. If I redesigned the assessment, I'd add questions about explaining why a change matters, plus space to give a real example for each rating. That's exactly what I've tried to do on this page.

The plan

How I'm closing the gaps.

Share the work

In my next group project, assign clear roles from the start and trust teammates to own their parts instead of doing everything myself.

Explain it like a coach

Practice explaining each AI concept I learn in one simple sentence and one everyday example, the way I'd teach a beginner on the court.

Turn knowledge into experience

Lead a small AI initiative, such as introducing a new AI tool to my team, so my ratings reflect what I've done, not just what I know.

Reassess with feedback

Repeat this assessment each term, and ask a supervisor and a classmate to rate me too, so the results aren't based on my view alone.

What this means for your team.

AI changes how people work, and that change only succeeds when people understand it and trust it. I bring calm adaptability, a fairness-first mindset, and a habit of asking "why" before "how." Add a clear plan to grow as a leader, and that's someone who can help your team adopt AI well.
